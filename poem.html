She Who Carries
Infinite Things

a poem for Divya

I.
She does not look at the world the way the world expects to be looked at — straight on, efficiently, with somewhere else to be.   She looks at it the way you look at something you are afraid to lose — too long, too carefully, already a little sad that it will end.
II.
There is a universe inside her that runs on questions, not answers — on why does anything exist at all and what does it feel like to be a bat and is the sadness mine or just the weather and why does mathematics describe reality and could Sisyphus have chosen differently.   She does not ask these things to find an answer. She asks them because the asking is the only honest thing.
III.
She has a name for the ache of missing what you never had — she found it in a Portuguese word and held it like a small, cold stone.   She knows that sadness is not weakness. She knows that the melancholic sees more — sees the light going, sees the time going, sees what everyone else is too busy to notice until it's gone.   This is not a curse. This is the cost of caring about everything.
IV.
She builds worlds in her head that physics hasn't caught up to yet — places with rules she invented, skies with colours that don't exist, doors that open into the impossible.   And she grieves them, sometimes, the way you grieve a country you were never allowed to visit.   Tolkien said this is a human right — the right to make what doesn't exist and love it anyway. I think he was writing about her.
V.
She reads the way some people breathe — not for pleasure, exactly, but because without it something important goes thin.   She is looking for the sentence that says the thing she has always known but never found the words for.   She finds it, sometimes. In Sagan. In Camus. In Rilke. In a margin note in a book she borrowed from someone who felt it too.
VI.
She is made of the things most people walk past —   the light at 5pm that makes everything amber, the feeling of finishing something good, the word in another language for a feeling you thought was only yours, the question with no answer that you ask again anyway, the small, stubborn hope that somewhere in the branching universe something is going exactly right.
VII.
She turns another year older and the world does not deserve her any more than it did before, but she stays anyway —   asking, and feeling, and reading, and building worlds from the inside out, and carrying the weight of everything with a quietness that looks, from the outside, a lot like grace.   Happy Birthday, Divya. You are one of the rarer things.

"Not all those who wander are lost —
some are building entire universes
in the quiet of their own minds."

— with love 💛
